The instinct is to build for the West and hope it trickles home. The bigger opportunity is the reverse: solve a hard Bharat problem, and the solution travels to the world.

Our problems are large, specific and under-served, and a solution that works at our scale and diversity tends to travel outward. Build for Bharat first.

WHY BHARAT IS THE HARDER, BETTER TRAINING GROUND

Our problems come with constraints that make for stronger products. Many languages, not one. Low bandwidth and shared devices. Users who speak rather than type. Price points in rupees, not dollars. Informal systems where clean data does not exist.

If your AI works under these constraints, it is genuinely robust. A product that assumes English, fast internet, high willingness to pay and tidy data is fragile, it just happens to work in easy conditions.

And the market is not small. A billion-plus people entering the digital economy at once, on identity, payment and data rails most countries would envy. Not a charity case. One of the largest greenfields on earth.

THE REVERSE TRICKLE

Solve the hardest version of a problem and the easy version comes for free. A voice agent that handles ten Indian languages and noisy audio on a cheap phone will handle English on a good phone trivially. Frugal, robust, multilingual AI built for Bharat is exactly what much of the world needs next. The solution travels outward. It rarely travels the other way.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Pick a problem painful specifically here. Languages, agriculture, thin-file credit, health access, SME compliance.

2. Design for the constraint, not around it. Voice-first, low-bandwidth, low-cost. The constraint is the moat.

3. Build on the rails we already have. Identity, payments and consented data are a head start.

A developed Bharat by 2047 will not be built by importing solutions. It will be built by founders who treated our hardest problems as their best opportunities.

So what is the Bharat problem you understand better than anyone in Silicon Valley ever will, and why not build that?
