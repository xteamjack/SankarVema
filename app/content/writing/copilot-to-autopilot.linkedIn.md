Every AI use case travels a curve from copilot to autopilot. Knowing where each one sits, and when to remove the human, is the real operating skill.

Most enterprise AI today is a copilot: it suggests, drafts, recommends, and a human decides. That is the safe start, and also a waypoint, not a destination.

THE CURVE

- Suggest. The AI proposes, the human does everything. Max safety, min leverage.
- Draft. The AI produces the work, the human reviews. Most value lives here today.
- Act with approval. The AI does the task and executes on a yes. The human becomes an approver.
- Autopilot. The AI acts within defined bounds, and the human is pulled in only on exceptions.

Value climbs at every stage. So does risk, if you move before you have earned it.

WHEN TO MOVE UP

You earn the next stage with evidence, not enthusiasm. The signal is boring: a track record. When the human review has become a rubber stamp, you are paying a person to approve what the machine already gets right. That is the moment to move up, and to spend the freed attention on the exceptions.

Move too fast and you automate an error at scale. Stay too slow and you cap the value at "a slightly faster human," and pay for a reviewer forever.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Place every use case on the curve. Most teams cannot say what stage they are at.

2. Define promotion criteria in advance. What accuracy, over what period, earns the next stage. A gate, not a vibe.

3. Design the exception path first. Autopilot is only safe when the handoff to a human is clean and fast.

The goal is not a human in every loop forever. It is moving the human from doing the work to governing it, one earned step at a time.

So for your top AI use case: what stage is it at, and what would earn it the next one?
