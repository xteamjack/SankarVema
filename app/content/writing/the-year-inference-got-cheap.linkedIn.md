The most important number in AI has been falling off a cliff, and most strategies have not caught up.

The cost of a unit of machine reasoning, a token, has dropped by more than an order of magnitude in a short span, and it is still falling. Reasoning models that think before they answer arrived at the same time. Thinking got better and cheaper at once. Most enterprise AI plans were written when it was still expensive. They are now out of date.

WHY THIS CHANGES THE PLAN

When intelligence is expensive, you ration it. Use it only on the high-value calls, keep prompts short, never let a model loop. Every one of those instincts becomes wrong when the price collapses.

Cheap inference means you can let a system think longer, check its own work, try several approaches, and call a model many times inside one task. Verification, self-correction, multi-step reasoning: too expensive last year, just line items now. The question shifts from "can we afford AI here" to "why are we not using it everywhere it removes toil."

THE TRAP ON THE OTHER SIDE

Cheap per call is not cheap at scale. When one user action quietly triggers fifty model calls, the bill grows faster than the value. The discipline is no longer rationing. It is instrumentation. Track cost per outcome, not cost per token.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Revisit the use cases you killed for being too expensive. Your no-list from last year is your opportunity list this year.

2. Design for thinking, not just answering. The extra calls are cheap, the wrong answer is not.

3. Track cost per outcome. Falling unit prices hide rising totals.

The teams that win did not spend the least on tokens. They noticed the price moved, and rebuilt the plan around it.

So before your next roadmap: which of your assumptions were priced at last year's cost of thinking?
