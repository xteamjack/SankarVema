Yesterday I argued AI is running ahead of even the analysts who forecast it. Today, the harder question: can our organizations actually keep up?

Most cannot. And that is not a failure. It is physics.

ADOPTION IS NOT ABSORPTION

You can buy frontier intelligence in an afternoon. You cannot buy clean, ready data, redesigned processes, an eval discipline, change management, and human trust at the same speed. Those move at the pace of an organization, not a model release.

Pour more capability into a company that cannot digest it and you do not get more value. You get indigestion: a wall of pilots, shelfware, half-built systems no one owns, budgets that rise while results stay flat. Eating faster does not make you better nourished.

THE BOTTLENECK MOVED, AND NOBODY MOVED WITH IT

For two years the constraint was the model. Now the model is the easy part. The constraint quietly shifted to absorption: data, evaluation, governance, people. That layer did not get faster just because the models did.

This is why so much AI spend is motion without progress. The organization is asked to swallow capability faster than it can chew.

The question is not how fast can we adopt AI. It is how fast can we absorb it. You cannot shortcut that with a bigger model or a bigger budget.

So: what is your absorption rate, honestly? And are you funding capability far faster than the ability to digest it?
