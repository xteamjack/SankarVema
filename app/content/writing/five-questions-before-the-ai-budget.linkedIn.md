Most boards approve AI budgets on faith and a good demo. Five hard questions separate the programs that will compound from the ones that quietly disappear.

Ask them before the budget is approved, not after it is spent. Any CEO should welcome them. Any program that cannot answer them is not ready.

THE FIVE QUESTIONS

1. What number does this move, and in what unit? If the answer is "efficiency" or "innovation," it is not a business case. Demand dollars, hours, risk, revenue, cycle time. A number you did not name is one you cannot defend.

2. Are we building an asset we own, or renting a capability everyone rents? If the advantage is a general model on a generic workflow, every competitor buys the same thing. What here is ours, that a rival cannot buy tomorrow?

3. What is the plan for year two? AI value compounds in the second year: the platform, the data flywheel, the eval loop. Funded for a quarter, expected to pay off for a decade, is mis-designed.

4. Who owns it when the pilot ends? Most AI dies in the gap between a successful pilot and a production owner. Which team runs this, on which platform, with what budget, after the excitement fades?

5. What could go wrong, and who is accountable? Governance, data, model error, vendor lock-in. A program without a named owner of its downside is an incident waiting to be scheduled.

WHY THESE FIVE

Each kills a specific way AI money gets wasted: unnamed outcomes, rented advantage, quarter-thinking, orphaned pilots, unowned risk. Answer all five and you are honest and fundable. Fail them and you will absorb budget and produce slides.

So before the next AI budget lands: which of these five can your program not yet answer?
