For thirty years, software meant buttons. The next decade, it means goals.

You used to click, and the app responded. You supplied the intent, the software supplied the mechanism. Agentic AI flips that. You state the goal, and the software works out the steps. That is not a feature. It is a new category.

FROM MECHANISM TO INTENT

An app waits to be told what to do, one step at a time. An agent is handed an outcome and decides the steps itself, calling tools, reading data, retrying when it fails. The interface stops being a screen full of controls and becomes a conversation about what you want.

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES

1. The unit of work changes. You no longer design screens, you design goals, tools and guardrails. The screen becomes optional.

2. The failure mode changes. A button either works or it does not. An agent can do the wrong thing, confidently. Reliability becomes a design problem, not a test at the end.

3. The org changes. The question stops being "which app does the team use" and becomes "which work does the team still own."

THE TRAP

Bolting an agent onto an existing app is not transformation. That is a chatbot in front of the same buttons. Redesign the workflow around the goal: what the agent owns, what the human owns, and where the hard stop lives. An agent without a guardrail is not autonomy. It is liability.

The winners of the next decade will not have the most AI features. They will have worked out which work to hand to an agent, and which to keep.

So, for your product and your org: which of your buttons should have been a goal?
