You cannot bolt AI onto an org designed for humans and get AI-native results. But you also do not need a reorg circus. Here is the small structural change that works.

Most AI transformations fail not at the model but at the org chart. A dramatic reorganization is not the answer either, those consume a year and produce mostly anxiety.

WHY THE CURRENT ORG RESISTS AI

A traditional org is built around people doing tasks, grouped into functions. AI cuts across that. It needs a shared platform, data and evals that no single function owns. Left to the default structure, every function builds its own AI in isolation, re-solving the same problems badly, and nothing reaches production.

THE FOUR ROLES THAT MAKE IT WORK

1. A small central AI platform team. Owns the shared substrate: model gateway, eval harness, observability, governance tooling. Small, senior, product-minded, not a committee.

2. Embedded AI engineers in the business lines. They build the use cases, close to the domain, consuming the platform instead of rebuilding it.

3. Domain experts paired with engineers, not parked on an advisory board. The person who knows the workflow sits with the person who builds it.

4. A governance function with real teeth. Owns risk, data and standards, and can say no. A guardrail that lets everyone else move faster, safely.

WHAT DOES NOT WORK

A Center of Excellence that owns no product is a budget line, not a structure. And fully decentralized AI produces forty half-built systems and no platform. The winning shape is central substrate, distributed building.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Stand up a small platform team before the use cases, not after.

2. Embed builders in the business, do not centralize all AI work.

3. Pair experts with engineers, and give governance real authority.

You do not need to redraw the whole org chart. You need to add four boxes and connect them well.

So: does your AI platform exist as a team, or is every function quietly rebuilding the same thing alone?
