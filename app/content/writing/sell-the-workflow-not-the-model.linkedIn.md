AI startups that resell intelligence get commoditized the day the next model ships. The ones that own an end-to-end workflow keep the value.

Two kinds of AI startup. One sells access to intelligence: a thin wrapper that passes your request to a model and the answer back. The other sells a workflow: it owns a whole job end to end, and the model is just one part inside.

WHY THE WRAPPER LOSES

A wrapper's advantage is the model, and the model is not yours. When a better or cheaper one arrives, your differentiation evaporates and your customer can rebuild it in a weekend. You are renting your entire value proposition from a vendor whose price and roadmap you do not control. That is a feature waiting to be absorbed.

WHY THE WORKFLOW WINS

A workflow product owns what a model cannot: the integrations, the domain steps, the accumulated data, the guarantees on the outcome, the ugly edge cases. The model can improve or be swapped underneath, and the product only gets better. The customer is not buying intelligence, they are buying a job done. That compounds, because every customer's usage makes the workflow smarter.

THE TEST

If the model I use became free and perfect tomorrow, do I still have a business? If no, you are selling the model. If yes, because you own the workflow, the data and the outcome, you have something.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Own the end-to-end job, not a step. Wrap the integration and edge cases the model will not do.

2. Accumulate proprietary data as a byproduct. Every run should make you harder to copy.

3. Sell the outcome, priced on value, not tokens. Customers buy results, not inference.

The model is a commodity input, and it is getting cheaper. The workflow around it is the product.

So if your model became free tomorrow, would you still have a company?
