You do not need an AI strategy with forty initiatives. You need the three workflows where AI moves a number the CFO recognizes, and you need to do those first.

Most AI strategies fail by being too ambitious. Forty initiatives, a committee, a platform, no clear first win. The teams that succeed find three workflows that matter and do those, well, before anything else.

THE FILTER

A workflow is worth doing when it scores on three things at once:

- Volume. It happens a lot. Small gains compound only on a large base.
- Pain. It is manual, slow, or error-prone today, so the improvement is felt.
- Boundedness. Defined enough for a machine to do reliably, with a clear right answer or a clear place to stop.

Miss any one and the case gets shaky. High volume but unbounded is a reliability nightmare. Painful but rare is not worth the build. You want all three.

THE NUMBER TEST

Can you name the outcome in a unit the CFO recognizes? Hours, dollars, error rate, cycle time, revenue. If the best you can say is "efficiency," it is not one of your three. Not yet.

THE METHOD

1. List your highest-volume operational processes. Start where the people and the tickets are.

2. Score each on volume, pain, and boundedness. Be honest about boundedness, teams overrate it.

3. Attach a number to the top few. If you cannot, dig until you can, or drop it.

4. Pick three. Not thirty. Three you can ship and prove in two quarters.

Three proven workflows build the credibility, the platform, and the appetite for the next ten. Forty parallel pilots build a graveyard.

Strategy is choosing. So which three workflows would you bet the whole AI program on, and why not start there?
