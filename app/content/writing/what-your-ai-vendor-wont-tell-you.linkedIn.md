The demo is honest. The contract is where the real story lives. Five things your enterprise AI vendor is not eager to explain before you sign.

Your vendor is not lying. They are answering the questions you ask, and the important ones do not come up in the demo.

FIVE THINGS TO ASK ABOUT

1. The cost at scale, not the pilot. The pilot is cheap by design. Ask the fully loaded cost per outcome at your real volume, including the model calls one user action triggers. Pilots are priced to get you in.

2. What happens to your data. Is it used to train their models, is it isolated, can you get it back, and does their platform become the only place your data and evals live? If your moat ends up inside their system, that is the real price.

3. How hard it is to leave. What does it take to switch models or vendors in eighteen months? If you are tied to their weights, format or proprietary layer, you are buying a dependency, and they know it.

4. Who owns the accuracy. When the model is wrong in production, whose problem is it? Vendors own the demo and quietly disown the edge cases. Ask where their responsibility ends, before it matters.

5. Where the price goes next. You do not control the price of the intelligence you rent. What has happened to their pricing, and what leverage do you have if it moves against you?

THE PATTERN

Every one is about who captures the value in three years. Their goal is to be indispensable and your switching costs high. Not villainy, strategy. Own your data, keep the model swappable, price on outcomes, and read the exit clause as carefully as the feature list.

So before your next AI contract: which of these five did the vendor not bring up, and why do you think that is?
