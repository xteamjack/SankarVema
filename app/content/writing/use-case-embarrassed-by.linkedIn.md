The AI use case that pays for itself is rarely the one on the keynote slide. It is the dull, high-volume, back-office chore nobody wants to present.

Ask a leadership team for their flagship AI use case and you hear something impressive: a customer assistant, a strategy copilot, a moonshot. Ask which one actually paid for itself, and if they are honest, it is something they are almost embarrassed to mention. Reconciling invoices. Reading claims. Tagging tickets.

WHY THE BORING WINS

The value of an AI use case is roughly volume times friction times repeatability. The flashy ones are low volume, high stakes, full of edge cases, exactly where AI is hardest to trust. The unglamorous ones are the opposite: huge volume, painful manual friction, and a task bounded enough for a machine to do reliably. That is the sweet spot.

A process run ten thousand times a day, each time costing a person a few minutes of tedium, is a bigger prize than a dazzling assistant used twice a week. It just does not demo well.

THE REASON IT GETS SKIPPED

Nobody gets promoted for automating the invoice queue. The prestige sits on the shiny front-end, so the highest-ROI work stays unfunded while the demo-friendly work takes the budget. That is a governance failure dressed up as a roadmap.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Follow the toil, not the glamour. List what people do the most and enjoy the least.

2. Score by volume times friction, not by visibility. Pick the one that moves a real number.

3. Ship it quietly, then show the result. A boring workflow in production saves more than ten demos.

The best AI use case in your company is probably one nobody wants to present. Fund it anyway.

So which of your workflows is too boring to demo, and too expensive to ignore?
