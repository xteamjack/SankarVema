The old build-vs-buy question has a third option now: rent. Knowing which parts of your AI stack to own and which to rent is the difference between a moat and a bill.

It is not one decision. It is a decision per layer, and most enterprises get it backwards.

THE THREE LAYERS

- The model. Almost always rent. The frontier moves too fast and costs too much to build your own, and a general model is not where your advantage lives. Rent it, keep it swappable.

- The workflow and product. Almost always build. Your process, your integrations, your edge cases. Where the value and defensibility live. Do not outsource your moat.

- The data and evals. Always own. Your proprietary data and the eval loop that improves your system are the compounding asset. Whoever owns this owns the advantage in three years. Never give it away inside a vendor's platform.

The most common and most expensive mistake is inverting this: renting the workflow (a generic tool everyone has) while giving away the data (into the vendor's system). That is renting your moat and selling your advantage, in one move.

THE TEST FOR EACH LAYER

Does this layer compound, and is it specific to us? Yes to both, own it. Fast-moving, capital-intensive, generic, rent it. Specific but not core, buy and integrate.

WHAT TO DO NOW

1. Rent the model, keep it swappable. Avoid lock-in to one vendor's weights.

2. Build the workflow and own the data. That is where durable return lives.

3. Watch for the inversion. If the pitch has you renting the workflow and feeding them your data, you are on the wrong side of every layer.

Build vs buy was always about where to own the compounding asset. Rent made it three-sided, and easier to get wrong.

So layer by layer: what are you building, what are you renting, and did you swap the two?
