If you want to find the highest-return AI project in a large enterprise, do not
look at the innovation lab. Look at the compliance department. KYC checks, claims
adjudication, audit prep, contract review, regulatory reporting. These
document-heavy, rule-bound processes are the least glamorous work in the building,
and that is exactly why they are the killer app for AI.

## Why compliance is the sweet spot

These workflows have every property that makes AI pay.

- **Volume.** They run constantly, at scale, across every customer or transaction.
- **Cost.** They are staffed by expensive, skilled people doing repetitive reading and cross-checking.
- **Structure.** The rules are written down. There is a right answer, and a defensible reason for it.
- **Pain.** They are slow, they bottleneck the business, and getting them wrong carries real regulatory risk.

AI that reads a document, extracts the facts, checks them against the rules, and
flags the exceptions turns a slow, costly, manual bottleneck into a fast,
consistent, auditable one. The value is immediate, and measurable in a unit the CFO
and the risk committee both recognize.

## The catch that is also the point

Compliance is high stakes, so it demands the discipline AI needs anyway:
traceability, citations, a clear audit trail, and a human on the exceptions. That
feels like friction. It is actually why compliance is a good first home for
enterprise AI. It forces you to build AI you can trust and explain, which is the
capability you will need everywhere else. You get a high-ROI win and build the
governance muscle at the same time.

## What to do now

1. **Map your regulated, document-heavy processes.** That map is a ranked list of
   AI opportunities hiding in plain sight.
2. **Demand traceability by design.** Every AI decision should cite the document
   and the rule it came from. In compliance that is not optional, and that is a feature.
3. **Keep the human on the exceptions, not the routine.** Let AI clear the ninety
   percent that is clear, and route the hard ten percent to your experts.

The most boring department in your company may be sitting on your best AI business
case. So which of your compliance bottlenecks is quietly the most expensive, and
why is it not first in line?
