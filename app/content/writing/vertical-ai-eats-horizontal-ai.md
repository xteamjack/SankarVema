The first wave of AI products was horizontal: one assistant, for everyone, for
everything. Impressive, and increasingly a commodity, because your competitor has
the same one. The durable value is moving the other way, into vertical AI:
systems that go deep into a single industry, carrying the data, the workflow, and
the judgment that only that domain has. Vertical eats horizontal.

## Why depth wins

A horizontal assistant knows a little about everything and owns nothing. A vertical
system knows one domain cold: its documents, its regulations, its edge cases, the
specific decisions its people make all day. That depth is exactly what a general
model cannot buy off the shelf, because it lives in proprietary data and hard-won
workflow, not in the model weights.

Depth also compounds. A vertical system that sees a domain's data and outcomes
every day gets sharper at that domain in a way a general tool never will. The moat
is not the model. It is the domain loop wrapped around it.

## The strategic read

For a buyer, this reframes build vs buy. A horizontal copilot is a rent: useful,
and identical to what everyone else rents. A vertical capability built on your
proprietary data and workflow is an asset: harder to build, and yours. If you want
advantage rather than parity, you fund depth.

For a builder, it says the same thing from the other side. Do not build a thinner
version of a general assistant. Pick one industry, one painful workflow, and own it
end to end. A narrow product a specialist cannot live without beats a broad product
they can take or leave.

## What to do now

1. **Ask what only you know.** Your proprietary data and domain workflow are the
   raw material of a moat. A general model is not.
2. **Prefer deep over broad.** One workflow owned completely beats ten touched shallowly.
3. **Judge AI products on domain fit, not feature lists.** The winner understands
   your industry's edge cases, not the one with the longest capability sheet.

Horizontal AI made intelligence a utility. Vertical AI is where you turn that
utility into an advantage.

So in your business: what does your industry know that a general model never will,
and who is building on it, you or someone else?
