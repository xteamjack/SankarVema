The default instinct for an ambitious founder here is to build for the West, chase
dollar customers, and hope it eventually helps at home. I would flip it. The
larger, more durable opportunity is to solve a hard Bharat problem first. Our
problems are large, specific, and under-served, and a solution that works at our
scale and diversity tends to travel to the rest of the world. Build for Bharat first.

## Why Bharat is the harder, better training ground

Our problems come with constraints that make for stronger products. Many
languages, not one. Low bandwidth and shared devices, not unlimited compute in
every pocket. Users who speak rather than type. Price points measured in rupees,
not dollars. Informal systems where clean data does not exist. If your AI works
under these constraints, it is genuinely robust. A product that assumes English,
fast internet, high willingness to pay, and tidy data is a fragile product that
happens to work in easy conditions.

And the market is not small. It is a billion-plus people entering the digital
economy at once, on a digital public infrastructure of identity, payments and data
rails that most countries would envy. That is not a charity case. It is one of the
largest greenfields on earth.

## The reverse trickle

Here is the part founders miss. Solve for the hardest version of a problem and the
easy version comes for free. A voice agent that handles ten Indian languages and
noisy audio on a cheap phone will handle English on a good phone trivially. Frugal,
robust, multilingual AI built for Bharat is exactly what much of the Global South,
and plenty of the West, will need next. The solution travels outward. It rarely
travels the other way.

## What to do now

1. **Pick a problem painful specifically here.** Languages, agriculture, credit for
   the thin-file borrower, healthcare access, small-business compliance.
2. **Design for the constraint, not around it.** Voice-first, low-bandwidth,
   low-cost, offline-tolerant. The constraint is the moat.
3. **Build on the rails we already have.** Identity, payments and consented data
   infrastructure are a head start most markets do not have.

A developed Bharat by 2047 will not be built by importing solutions. It will be
built by founders who treated our hardest problems as their best opportunities.

So what is the Bharat problem you understand better than anyone in Silicon Valley
ever will, and why not build that?
