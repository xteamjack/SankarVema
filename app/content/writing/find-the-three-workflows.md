Most AI strategies fail by being too ambitious. Forty initiatives, a committee, a
platform, and no clear first win. The teams that succeed do the opposite. They
find three workflows where AI moves a number the business already cares about, and
they do those, well, before anything else. Here is how to find your three.

## The filter

A workflow is worth doing when it scores on three things at once.

- **Volume.** It happens a lot. Small improvements compound only when the base is large.
- **Pain.** It is manual, slow, or error-prone today, so the improvement is felt.
- **Boundedness.** The task is defined enough that a machine can do it reliably,
  with a clear right answer or a clear place to stop and ask.

Miss any one and the case gets shaky. High volume but unbounded is a reliability
nightmare. Bounded and painful but rare is not worth the build. You want all three.

## The number test

Then apply the number test: can you name the outcome in a unit the CFO recognizes?
Hours saved, dollars, error rate, cycle time, revenue. If the best you can say is
"efficiency" or "better experience," it is not one of your three. Not yet.

## The method

1. **List your highest-volume operational processes.** Start from where the people
   and the tickets are, not from where the excitement is.
2. **Score each on volume, pain, and boundedness.** Be honest about boundedness.
   It is the one teams consistently overrate.
3. **Attach a number to the top few.** If you cannot, dig until you can, or drop it.
4. **Pick three.** Not thirty. Three you can ship and prove within two quarters.

The point of three is focus. Three shipped, proven workflows build the
credibility, the platform, and the appetite for the next ten. Forty parallel
pilots build a graveyard.

Strategy is choosing. So which three workflows would you bet the whole AI program
on, and why not start there?
