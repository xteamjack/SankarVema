Founders chase the exciting startup: the consumer app, the frontier model, the
demo that goes viral. The startup that quietly prints money is usually the
opposite: a services business, in a dull vertical, disguised as a product, where AI
does most of the work behind the scenes. Boring is a feature.

## The shape of it

Pick an unglamorous industry with expensive manual work: bookkeeping for small
firms, insurance claims processing, medical billing, legal document review, freight
paperwork. Today it is done by people, slowly. You wrap the whole job as a product,
and you use AI to do the bulk of the labor under the hood. The customer sees an
outcome and a price. You keep the margin between what they pay for the result and
what it costs you to produce it with AI doing most of the work.

## Why it works

Three reasons this beats the flashy play. The pain is proven, so you do not have to
create demand, it already exists as a line item. The buyer is unglamorous and
underserved, so incumbents are weak and general tools do not bother. And AI
genuinely changes the unit economics, so a job that used to need ten people now
needs two plus software, and that gap is your margin. It will not trend on social
media. It will pay salaries.

## The honest caveat

This is not passive. Owning the outcome means owning the edge cases, the errors, and
the customer relationship. It is a real operating business, not a magic API. But
that operating difficulty is also the moat. It is exactly what a wrapper cannot be
bothered to do, and exactly what keeps your margin from being competed away.

## What to do now

1. **Find a dull vertical with expensive manual work.** The more boring, the less
   competition.
2. **Sell the outcome, run AI underneath.** The customer buys the result, not the technology.
3. **Keep the human on the last mile.** Own the edge cases. That is where trust and
   margin both live.

The best AI business you can start this year is probably one you would be slightly
embarrassed to pitch at a party. So which boring, expensive, manual job would you
turn into a product?
