For thirty years, software meant buttons. You clicked, it responded. The user
supplied the intent, and the app supplied the mechanism. Agentic AI inverts
that. You state the goal, and the software works out the steps. That sounds like
a feature. It is actually a new category.

## From mechanism to intent

An app waits to be told what to do, one step at a time. An agent is handed an
outcome and decides the steps itself, calling tools, reading data, and retrying
when it fails. The interface stops being a screen full of controls and becomes a
conversation about what you want. That is why "agents are the new apps" is not a
slogan. It is a shift in where the intelligence sits, from the user's head into
the software.

## What actually changes

Three things break the old model.

1. **The unit of work changes.** You no longer design screens, you design goals,
   tools and guardrails. The screen becomes optional.
2. **The failure mode changes.** A button either works or it does not. An agent
   can do the wrong thing, confidently. Reliability becomes a design problem, not
   a testing step at the end.
3. **The org changes.** If a capable agent can carry a task end to end, the
   question is no longer "which app does the team use," it is "which work does the
   team still own." That is an operating-model question, and most companies have
   not asked it yet.

## The trap

The easy mistake is to bolt an agent onto an existing app and call it
transformation. That is a chatbot standing in front of the same buttons. The real
move is to redesign the workflow around the goal: decide what the agent owns, what
the human owns, and where the hard stop lives. An agent without a guardrail is not
autonomy. It is liability.

## What to do now

- Pick one bounded, high-volume workflow and design it goal-first, not screen-first.
- Give the agent a narrow tool surface and an explicit point where it must stop and ask.
- Measure it like a colleague, on outcomes and error rate, not on usage.

The companies that win the next decade will not be the ones with the most AI
features. They will be the ones that worked out which work to hand to an agent,
and which to keep.

So here is the question for your product and your org: which of your buttons
should have been a goal?
