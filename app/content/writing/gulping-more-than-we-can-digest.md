Yesterday I wrote that AI is running ahead of even the analysts who forecast it.
Today, the harder question. If the technology is this far ahead, can our
organizations actually keep up? I do not think most can, and I do not think that is
a failure. It is physics.

## Adoption is not absorption

You can buy frontier intelligence in an afternoon. You cannot buy the things that
turn it into value at the same speed: clean and ready data, redesigned processes, an
evaluation discipline, change management, and the plain human trust it takes for
people to rely on a machine. Those move at the pace of an organization, not the pace
of a model release.

Pour more capability into a company that cannot digest it and you do not get more
value. You get indigestion. A wall of pilots. Shelfware. Half-built systems no one
owns. Budgets that rise while the results stay flat. Eating faster does not make you
better nourished.

## The bottleneck moved, and nobody moved with it

For two years the constraint was the model. Now the model is the easy part. The
constraint has quietly shifted to absorption, the unglamorous layer of data,
evaluation, governance, and people. That layer did not get faster just because the
models did. If anything, the faster the models move, the more exposed that layer becomes.

This is why so much AI spend produces motion without progress. The organization is
being asked to swallow capability faster than it can chew, and the gap shows up as
pilots that never ship and value that never lands.

## What a serious leader asks instead

The question is not how fast can we adopt AI. Everyone can adopt fast, that is the
easy part now. The question is how fast can we absorb it. That is set by your data
readiness, your platform, your eval loop, and your people, and you cannot shortcut
it with a bigger model or a bigger budget.

So before the next wave of AI you bring in: what is your absorption rate, honestly,
and are you funding capability far faster than you are funding the ability to digest it?
